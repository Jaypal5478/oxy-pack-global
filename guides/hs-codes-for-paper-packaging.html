HS Codes for Paper Packaging: A Guide for Importers

Every shipment of packaging you import needs an HS code, and the code decides which duty rate and rules apply. This guide explains how HS codes work for paper packaging and maps common products, from corrugated boxes to paper cups, to their usual headings.

What is an HS code?

The Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System, usually called the HS, is an international product classification maintained by the World Customs Organization. More than 200 countries and economies use it as the basis for their customs tariffs and trade statistics.

Every product you import is classified under an HS code. Customs authorities use that code to decide the import duty rate, any taxes, trade agreement benefits, and whether licences or other controls apply. Getting it right avoids delays, penalties and unexpected costs.

How HS codes are structured

An HS code is read from left to right, becoming more specific at each level:

  • Chapter (2 digits): for example, Chapter 48 covers paper and paperboard and articles of paper pulp, paper or paperboard.
  • Heading (4 digits): for example, 4819 covers cartons, boxes, cases, bags and other packing containers of paper.
  • Subheading (6 digits): for example, 4819.10 covers cartons, boxes and cases of corrugated paper or paperboard.

The first six digits are the same in every country that uses the HS. Individual countries then add further digits for their own tariff and statistical purposes:

  • India uses the 8-digit ITC-HS classification for exports and imports.
  • The United States uses the 10-digit Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS).
  • The European Union uses the 8-digit Combined Nomenclature (CN), with further digits in the TARIC database for specific measures.
  • The UK, GCC states, Australia and African customs unions each publish their own national tariffs built on the same 6-digit base.

This is why the code on your supplier's export invoice may have a different number of digits from the code your customs broker declares at import. The first six digits should normally match.

HS code 4819: boxes, cartons and bags of paper

Heading 4819 is the main home for paper packaging. Its subheadings are:

  • 4819.10: cartons, boxes and cases of corrugated paper or paperboard. This is the HS code for corrugated boxes, including shipping cartons, mailer boxes, pizza boxes and heavy-duty export cartons.
  • 4819.20: folding cartons, boxes and cases of non-corrugated paper or paperboard. This covers printed folding cartons made from duplex, SBS or similar board.
  • 4819.30: sacks and bags having a base of a width of 40 cm or more. This typically covers multiwall paper sacks for cement, flour, chemicals and animal feed.
  • 4819.40: other sacks and bags, including cones. This covers most paper carry bags, grocery bags, SOS bags and paper mailers with a base narrower than 40 cm.
  • 4819.50: other packing containers, including record sleeves. This can cover rigid set-up boxes and other containers that are not folding cartons.
  • 4819.60: box files, letter trays, storage boxes and similar articles, of a kind used in offices, shops or the like.

Note the base-width test for bags: the same style of paper bag can fall under 4819.30 or 4819.40 depending on its base dimension.

HS code 4823: paper cups, plates and moulded fibre

Heading 4823 covers other articles of paper, paperboard and paper pulp not specified elsewhere. For packaging buyers, the key subheadings are:

  • 4823.69: trays, dishes, plates, cups and the like, of paper or paperboard, other than of bamboo paper. This is the usual HS code for paper cups, paper bowls, soup containers and paper plates.
  • 4823.70: moulded or pressed articles of paper pulp. This covers moulded fibre tableware such as bagasse and pulp plates, bowls and clamshells, as well as moulded pulp trays and inserts.
  • 4823.90: other. Items such as paper straws and paper edge protectors are often classified here, but classification can vary between countries and depends on the exact product.

Other relevant codes: 4822, 4806 and 4808

  • 4822: bobbins, spools, cops and similar supports of paper pulp, paper or paperboard. Paper tubes and cores used for winding textiles, films, labels or other materials generally fall here.
  • 4806.20: greaseproof papers. Greaseproof wrapping paper supplied in rolls or sheets is usually classified here. If the paper is printed or cut into specific shapes for a final use, a different heading may apply.
  • 4808: paper and paperboard, corrugated, creped, crinkled, embossed or perforated, in rolls or sheets. Corrugated board in rolls or sheets, rather than made-up boxes, is classified under 4808.10.

Why duty rates differ by destination

The HS code is shared, but the duty rate attached to it is set by each importing country. The same 4819.10 carton can attract a different rate in the UAE, the EU, the UK, the USA, Australia or an African customs union. On top of the duty, VAT, GST or sales taxes may apply at import.

Trade agreements can reduce or remove duty when goods qualify as originating in India and a valid preferential certificate of origin is presented. Rules of origin, documentation and tariff schedules vary by agreement, so ask your broker whether a preference is available for your product and how to claim it.

Common classification mistakes

  • Corrugated versus non-corrugated. A printed box made from corrugated E flute board is 4819.10, not 4819.20, even though it may look like a folding carton.
  • Ignoring the bag base width. Bags with a base of 40 cm or more belong in 4819.30; narrower bags in 4819.40.
  • Confusing paperboard and pulp. Cups and bowls made from paperboard are 4823.69, while moulded pulp products are 4823.70.
  • Classifying by material instead of article. Board in sheets is 4808 or another paper heading, but once it becomes a box, the article heading 4819 applies.
  • Using a different code on the invoice and declaration. Mismatches can trigger customs queries. Agree the code with your supplier before shipping.

How to confirm the right code

  1. Describe the product precisely: material, construction, dimensions and intended use.
  2. Ask your supplier for the HS code they will declare on export documents.
  3. Share the description and code with your customs broker, who confirms the national tariff line at destination.
  4. For high-volume or unusual items, consider a binding tariff ruling from your customs authority.

What is the HS code for corrugated boxes?

Cartons, boxes and cases of corrugated paper or paperboard are classified under HS 4819.10. Your country adds further digits for its own tariff line.

What is the HS code for paper cups?

Paper cups are usually classified under HS 4823.69, which covers trays, dishes, plates, cups and similar articles of paper or paperboard other than bamboo.

What does HS code 4819 cover?

Heading 4819 covers cartons, boxes, cases, bags and other packing containers of paper, paperboard, cellulose wadding or webs of cellulose fibres, plus box files and similar office articles.

What is the HS code for paper bags?

Paper bags with a base width of 40 cm or more fall under 4819.30, while other paper bags, including most carry bags, usually fall under 4819.40.

Who decides the final HS code for my import?

The importer is responsible for correct classification, usually with the help of a customs broker. Codes in this guide are indicative and should be confirmed for your destination.
